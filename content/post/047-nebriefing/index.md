Earlier this week, I came across the National Emergency Briefings that are reported to be streamed across the UK over the past half a year. These concern the climate crisis, and how we are already in one. It's no longer in the future, but already in the present.

Without efforts comparable to and exceeding the world wars of the previous century, the effects in our own lifetimes could be far worse. Thor's The Blip might not be just fiction but awaiting us in a few decades. Scientists across the world are banging their heads how we might possibly steer out of this with minimal damage. But it can only happen when the top 1% of us who have contributed to about half the crisis pick up responsibility for it. Their share of burden exceeds twice that of the bottom 50%.

It's not merely a matter of the planet heating up, of temperatures changing by 1 or 2C. But these seemingly small changes having varying effects across the world, including the entire vanishing of the Arctic and Antarctic Ice, the change in climates across countries and continents, the intensifying of extreme events, all posing a risk to food and water resources that are critical to all of us. To put Antarctic into perspective: it is 40% larger than Europe, which in itself is comparable to China and 3 times larger than India.
